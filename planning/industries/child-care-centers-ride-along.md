# A Day in the Shoes of a Child Care Center Director

## 6:15 AM – Unlocking the front door

I'm always the first one here. Lights on, thermostat up, the infant room's sound machine switched on before anyone's feet hit the carpet. I check my phone in the parking lot like I do every morning, bracing for it.

There it is. A text from Marisol: *"so sorry, my daughter's fever came back, can't come in today."*

*Thinking: "That's my toddler room down to one. If nobody else calls out, I can still make ratio — barely."*

I pull up my on-call sub list. Two names. One's a former assistant who's back in school now, the other hasn't answered a text in three weeks. I text both anyway and start doing the math I do most mornings: eight toddlers, one teacher, I need a second body in that room by 7:30 or I'm capping enrollment for the day — turning away paying, already-enrolled families at drop-off.

---

## 6:52 AM – Coffee, cold, forgotten on the desk

I'm digging through the filing cabinet for the sub-teacher packet — emergency contacts, allergy list, daily schedule — because whoever walks in that door in an hour won't know any of it unless I hand it to them on paper.

*Thinking: "There should be one printed sheet an app spits out the second I mark someone absent, not fifteen minutes of me hunting for last month's version."*

My phone buzzes. A voicemail already, 6:50 AM — probably a parent. I let it go to voicemail on the voicemail. I can't call anyone back until drop-off is handled.

---

## 7:10 AM – Drop-off begins

Cars start pulling into the lot. I'm at the door doing temperature checks and sign-ins on the Brightwheel tablet while mentally tracking who's walked into which room. A dad hands off his daughter mid-tantrum, apologizes, says "she didn't sleep great," and I say what I always say — "she'll be laughing in ten minutes" — because usually she is.

By 7:40 the toddler room still only has one teacher. My back-up sub isn't answering. I make the call I hate making: I'm going in the room myself for the morning. That means the front desk, the phone, the tours — all of it sits unattended for as long as I'm in ratio.

*Thinking: "Every minute I'm in that room is a minute nobody's answering the phone, and somebody out there is deciding between us and the center down the street right now."*

---

## 8:35 AM – Three missed calls, one voicemail

I finally get five minutes at my desk. Three missed calls since 7 AM, one voicemail. It's a mom asking if we have any infant openings — she sounds like she's already called two other places. I write her name on a sticky note to call back at lunch.

*Thinking: "By the time I call her back, she's probably already toured somewhere else. That's not a maybe — that's just how this works now."*

---

## 9:15 AM – A scheduled tour, finally in person

A family I actually did manage to call back yesterday shows up for their tour. I walk them through the classrooms, point out the ratio posted on each door — not as a formality, it's the law, and parents notice when you know your numbers cold. I show them our license, framed by the entrance, current and clean.

They ask, "Do you have any openings for a two-year-old?" I have to tell them: waitlist, six to eight weeks, maybe sooner if someone moves. I watch their face do the math on whether to keep looking elsewhere too.

*Thinking: "I should be able to show her exactly where she'd land on that list right now, on a screen, instead of saying 'maybe' and hoping she believes me enough to wait."*

---

## 10:20 AM – The CCAP folder

Back at my desk, buried under it is the CCAP redetermination paperwork for two subsidized families — due to the state by Friday or their subsidy lapses and I eat the difference in tuition until it's resolved.

*Thinking: "That's potentially $1,400 of tuition I front for a family while the state catches up on its own paperwork."*

I also flip through the staff certification binder — CPR cards, background check renewals — because a licensing inspector could show up unannounced and one expired card is a citation.

---

## 11:10 AM – The tuition conversation

I have to text a parent who's now two weeks behind. I've sent the friendly reminder. Now it's the direct one: *"I need at least two weeks of tuition by Friday to keep her enrolled."* I hate sending it. I send it anyway, because the alternative is eating the cost of an empty seat that isn't actually empty.

*Thinking: "That's the third month in a row I've had to be the collections department. I became a director to work with kids, not to chase invoices."*

---

## 12:05 PM – Lunch at the desk, half in ratio

I eat a sandwich standing in the doorway of the preschool room because their teacher's on her lunch break and I'm covering. Between bites I return the voicemail from 8:35 AM.

She already enrolled somewhere else.

*Thinking: "There it is. That's a family, gone, because nobody could answer a phone for four hours this morning. How much is that worth over a year? More than I want to add up right now."*

---

## 1:30 PM – Naptime, my only quiet window

Naptime is when I do everything I couldn't do all morning: payroll approvals, an email to a parent about a biting incident, ordering diapers because we're down to one case. I finally open the state licensing portal to check the status of a pending inspection request.

*Thinking: "This is the only forty minutes of my day that isn't reactive, and half of it just went to a portal that takes three clicks to do one thing."*

---

## 2:45 PM – A teacher needs to leave early

One of my afternoon teachers has a doctor's appointment she forgot to tell me about until now. I redo the ratio math for the third time today, move a floater from the infant room — which puts infants right at the ratio line — and mentally note that tomorrow morning I need to check my sub list again before I even open the door.

---

## 3:40 PM – Pickup begins

Same dance as drop-off, in reverse. I'm at the door again, watching the clock, because our contract says a $15 fee kicks in at 6:01 PM and I always feel like the bad guy enforcing it — but I also know what happens to my own hourly staff if I don't, because someone has to stay until every last child is picked up.

A dad rolls in at 6:08 PM, apologetic, and I have to have the fee conversation again.

*Thinking: "That's the same conversation, different parent, probably twice a week. I shouldn't have to be the one saying it out loud every time."*

---

## 6:20 PM – Locking up

Building's empty. I do a final walkthrough — cribs, cots, the sign-in sheets that need to get logged into the system before tomorrow. I check tomorrow's staffing one more time. Marisol's still out sick, no confirmation from anyone on the sub list.

*Thinking: "I'm going to wake up tomorrow and do this exact same scramble before I've even had coffee."*

---

## 6:45 PM – Driving home

I run the tally I run every night without meaning to: one enrollment gone because nobody answered the phone this morning, one tuition conversation I'll have to follow up on again Friday, CCAP paperwork still not finished, a sub list that failed me today and probably will again tomorrow.

*Thinking: "None of today was about the kids not being fine. The kids were fine. It's everything around them that's held together by sticky notes and me being in three places at once."*

---

## THE DREAM SYSTEM

If I could design the perfect system for my center, here's what it would do.

### Before the day starts:
- The second I mark a teacher absent, it auto-texts my entire approved sub list at once — not me manually texting two people and hoping
- It shows me tonight, not tomorrow morning, exactly what ratio looks like in every room for the next day based on who's confirmed
- The sub-teacher info packet (allergies, emergency contacts, daily schedule) is a link I send, not a folder I dig for

### While I'm slammed during the day:
- Every tour inquiry and phone call becomes a form a parent fills out themselves — with real-time waitlist position by room — so a missed call doesn't automatically mean a lost family
- One screen shows me live ratio status for every room, so I'm not doing math in my head between diaper changes
- Tuition reminders and late notices go out automatically before I have to be the one to send them personally

### End of day:
- CCAP and subsidy paperwork status is tracked with real deadlines and reminders, not a folder I flip through hoping I remembered everything
- Staff certifications and licensing documents flag themselves before they expire, not after an inspector finds them
- Tomorrow's staffing gap, if there is one, is visible tonight — not discovered at 6:15 AM in the parking lot

### Old way vs. dream system:
**Old way:**
- A missed call at 8:35 AM quietly becomes a lost enrollment by lunch, and I don't even find out until I finally call back
- I personally chase every late tuition payment and every certification deadline, on top of running classrooms short-staffed
- Every family on the waitlist gets a vague "maybe soon" instead of an actual number

**Dream system:**
- Every inquiry gets an instant, accurate answer about openings and waitlist position — with or without me answering the phone
- Payment reminders, subsidy deadlines, and certification renewals run themselves in the background
- I spend naptime on the kids and the classroom, not on a portal

That's a realistic day in a child care center director's shoes — and exactly where an AI-powered solution slots in.

# Industry Download — Child Care Centers

## 1. Industry Overview

- **Number of businesses in the US:** ~591,000 day care businesses (IBISWorld 2026); ~675,000 total establishments when home-based family child care is included
- **Average annual revenue per business:** Industry-wide market size is $60.4B–$72.8B depending on scope; owner take-home for a center with ~36 kids enrolled runs $60K–$100K/year (total center revenue is higher — that figure is owner income after payroll, the largest cost line)
- **Average employee count:** Varies hugely by center size — a single-location center with 3-4 classrooms typically runs 8–15 staff (teachers + assistants + director)
- **Recession-proof?** Sort-of. Demand is inelastic — parents need care to work — but centers themselves are financially fragile: thin margins, payroll is 50-70% of costs, and staffing shortages force centers to turn away revenue they could otherwise collect.
- **2026 reality check:**
  - Demand still outstrips supply nationally — a widely cited figure is a 28% gap between available licensed slots and need. In more than half of Texas counties, children under 5 outnumber licensed slots 3:1.
  - Staffing is the industry's core operating constraint, not marketing or software. Over 80% of centers report an active staffing shortage; average annual staff turnover sits around 33% (median 23%), and each turnover event costs a center close to $7,000 in recruiting/onboarding/lost productivity.
  - Centers are forced to operate under capacity or cut hours specifically because they can't staff a classroom to legal ratio — meaning empty, paid-for seats sit unfilled not because families aren't asking, but because there's no legal way to accept them that day.
  - Tuition is rising nationally ($925–$1,230/month depending on age group) while state subsidy reimbursement (CCAP) lags, creating a cash-flow gap between when a subsidized family enrolls and when the center actually gets paid.
  - Because demand outpaces supply almost everywhere, this is currently a seller's market for centers — but that makes a fast, professional first response (tour booking, waitlist visibility) even more valuable, since families are actively comparing multiple centers' response speed while deciding where to sit on 3-4 waitlists at once.

## 3. Pain Points Ranked by Money Lost

1. **Staff turnover** — ~$7,000 per departure, average center turnover ~33%/year. A 10-staff center losing 3 staff/year is $21K/year in pure replacement cost, before counting lost productivity while a classroom runs short.
2. **Empty seats from being short-staffed, not short on demand** — a single 8-child classroom that can't open because it's not in legal ratio is $8,000+/month in tuition sitting on the table in a market where waitlisted families are actively asking.
3. **Waitlist mismanagement** — in a market with 6-12 month infant waitlists in some states, a family who doesn't hear back fast simply enrolls at the next center on their list. Every unanswered inquiry is a lost enrollment, not a delayed one.
4. **Subsidy (CCAP) reimbursement lag** — state child care assistance payments run behind actual enrollment by weeks to months, creating real cash-flow gaps for centers with a meaningful subsidized-family caseload.
5. **Tuition collection friction** — chasing late payments by phone/text/paper invoice instead of autopay; every manual follow-up is director/admin time not spent on enrollment or classroom support.
6. **No online tour/waitlist booking** — inquiries route to a front-desk phone call during the exact hours staff are in ratio with children and can't answer; calls go to voicemail, family moves to the next center on their list.
7. **Licensing/ratio compliance recordkeeping** — staff certifications, immunization records, and ratio logs tracked on paper risk real fines and, in serious cases, license action if a citation is missed.
8. **Manual daily parent communication** — centers without a digital daily-report tool (photos, meals, naps) burn staff time on handwritten slips and field a higher volume of "how was their day" calls/texts.
9. **DIY website with no integration to their actual operating system** — a Wix/Squarespace site is a second subscription that doesn't talk to whatever CCMS (Procare, Brightwheel, etc.) the center already uses for attendance/billing, so nothing about a tour request or application flows automatically into daily operations.
10. **Local search invisibility** — "daycare near me" and "[city] child care center" are high-intent searches; a center with a thin, non-optimized site loses free organic inquiries to competitors who show up first.

## 4. Language Dictionary

**Industry Terms:**
- **Ratio** — the legally mandated number of children one staff member may supervise (varies by age: infants ~1:3-1:4, toddlers ~1:3-1:5, preschool ~1:8-1:18). "In ratio" / "out of ratio" is said constantly and is a legal compliance term, not a soft guideline.
- **CCAP** — Child Care Assistance Program, the state-administered subsidy that pays part of tuition for income-eligible families
- **CCDF** — Child Care Development Fund, the federal funding mechanism that flows down into each state's CCAP-equivalent program
- **QRIS** — Quality Rating and Improvement System, the state-level star/tier rating many centers are scored on and market themselves by
- **Licensing** — the state agency and process governing whether a center can legally operate; "in good standing" vs. "a citation" are meaningfully different statuses
- **Accreditation** — voluntary, higher bar than licensing (NAEYC accreditation is the best-known national one)
- **DVI** (do not use) — not a childcare term; this is a dental industry acronym and using it would flag you as an outsider instantly
- **Infant / Toddler / Preschool / School-age room** — classrooms are referred to by the age band they serve, not by a generic "class"
- **Director** — the on-site operations lead, distinct from the owner (small centers the owner is often also the director)
- **Subsidy family** — a family whose tuition is partially paid via CCAP, distinct from "private pay"

**Acronyms:**
- **CCAP** — Child Care Assistance Program
- **CCDF** — Child Care Development Block Grant / Fund
- **QRIS** — Quality Rating and Improvement System
- **DCFS** — Department of Children and Family Services (the licensing body in several states — name varies by state)

**Pain Phrases (representative of documented operator sentiment):**
- "I had to close the infant room again because I don't have anyone to put in ratio."
- "I'm still waiting on this month's CCAP reimbursement and payroll is due Friday."
- "I've called that family back three times about the waitlist spot and they already enrolled somewhere else."
- "I need at least two weeks of tuition by Friday or I have to start the termination process."

**Success Phrases:**
- "We're fully staffed and full-ratio in every room."
- "Our waitlist is finally moving instead of just growing."
- "Tuition came in on autopay, nobody had to chase anything this month."

**Outsider Red Flags:**
| Say This (Wrong) | Say This Instead |
|---|---|
| "clients" | "families" |
| "students" (for infants/toddlers) | "children" or "kids" |
| "class size limit" | "ratio" |
| "daycare" as the formal business term in front of an owner (many prefer it, some consider it dated/dismissive vs. "child care center" or "early learning center") | ask/use whatever term is on their own signage and license |

## 5. What They Already Pay For

| Tool | Monthly Cost | Purpose | Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brightwheel | ~$200-300/mo at 40 kids | Parent communication, billing, attendance (market leader) | Priced by enrollment tier; doesn't solve public-facing marketing/tour booking |
| Procare | ~$85/mo at 40 kids | Center management, billing, attendance | Cheaper than Brightwheel but same gap — no public website/lead-capture layer |
| HiMama / Lillio | Per-child pricing | Daily reports, parent app | Same category as above — internal ops, not front-door marketing |
| Kangarootime | ~$24-48/mo per classroom | Center management software | Pricing scales with classrooms, resented as programs grow |
| Daily Connect | $8.99-12.99/mo (small programs) | Daily reports, online enrollment forms, waitlist | Budget option for small/home-based providers |
| Kiddo / WaitlistPlus / SchedulingKit | Free-$ | Waitlist and tour-booking specifically | These exist but are bolt-on point solutions, not the center's actual website |
| Wix / Squarespace | $16-$40/mo | DIY public website | Separate subscription from the CCMS above; doesn't integrate, requires manual double entry |

This is the exact gap: every dollar above goes toward running the center once a family is already enrolled. Almost nothing goes toward the moment a prospective family first finds the center online and decides whether to call.

## 7. Dream Outcome

A family finds the center from a "daycare near me" search, sees real photos of the actual classrooms, checks live availability by age group, books a tour online without a phone call, and submits an enrollment application before ever speaking to staff. The director's first conversation with them is a warm welcome tour, not an intake interview conducted from scratch over the phone. The waitlist manages itself and emails families automatically when a spot opens. Tuition runs on autopay. Zero phone tag, zero lost inquiries, zero "did you get my message."

**They'd pay $10K for:** A front door that converts every "are you taking new kids" search into a booked tour and a submitted application — without adding to the director's ratio-constrained day.

## 8. Market Gaps

1. **Childcare-native waitlist/tour booking built into the website itself** — not a bolt-on third-party widget disconnected from the site's design and SEO
2. **Live availability by age-group room** — most sites don't show whether the infant room specifically has an opening, which is the single question every inquiring family actually has
3. **Digital enrollment application intake** — pre-collects the information a director currently gathers on a phone call, so the first live conversation is a tour, not an interview
4. **License/accreditation trust signaling built into the site design** — license number, QRIS rating, and NAEYC accreditation (if any) prominently and credibly displayed, not buried in a footer
5. **Local SEO built for "daycare near me" / "[city] child care" intent** — most DIY sites are invisible here despite this being the highest-intent search a center will ever receive

## 9. The Perfect Software Solution

**Core problem to solve first:** Turning a "do you have an opening" search into a booked tour and a submitted application, without adding admin burden to a director who is already stretched thin by staffing and ratio compliance.

**MVP Features:**
1. Live per-room (age-group) availability + waitlist signup, shown directly on the site
2. Online tour booking with automated confirmation/reminder
3. Digital enrollment application intake form (replaces the phone-call interview)
4. License number, accreditation, and QRIS rating displayed as trust signals in the design itself
5. Mobile-first design tuned for "daycare near me" local search

**Phase 2:**
- Automated waitlist-position email updates ("you're now #2 for the toddler room")
- Multi-location dashboard for owners running more than one center
- Handoff integration guidance into whatever CCMS the center already runs (Procare/Brightwheel/etc.) so a booked tour or submitted application doesn't require re-entry
- Staff bios / classroom photo galleries that are easy for a director to update without touching code

**Pricing recommendation:**
- $97-147/month (in line with what centers already pay per-tool for Brightwheel/Procare-style software)
- ROI pitch: "One enrollment saved from going to a competitor because you answered first pays for a year of this."

# A Day in the Life — Wedding Venue Owner/Coordinator

## 6:47 AM — The Inbox

Sarah's phone buzzes before her alarm. She's the owner-operator of Fieldstone Manor, a 12-acre
event venue in the Texas Hill Country that books 80–90 weddings a year. She grabs the phone off
the nightstand. Three new inquiry form submissions came in overnight from WeddingWire.

She opens the first one. *October 2027. 250 guests. "Budget flexible."* She's seen this before.

*Flexible usually means they're flexible until they see the number.*

The second is for a February date — a Saturday. Good. She flags it mentally as hot. The third
is for a Sunday in January. Last year she couldn't fill three January Sundays.

She'll reply to all three before her first coffee. It's 6:47 AM.

**Dollar leakage thread begins here.** Three inquiries. Zero pre-qualification. No indication of
budget. Sarah will spend 35 minutes this morning crafting personalized replies. Two of these
couples will ghost her by Wednesday.

---

## 8:15 AM — The Morning Tour Prep

The first tour is at 10 AM. Couple from Austin — 150 guests, tentative date in April. Sarah's
coordinator, Megan, is already at the venue setting up the bridal suite for the walkthrough.
They've prepped this space for 11 AM and 2 PM tours as well.

*Three tours today. Saturday. We'd better not get no-shows.*

Last Saturday, two out of four tours didn't show. No call, no email, just silence. One couple
texted three hours later: "Sorry, we decided to go with a venue closer to home." The other never
contacted them at all.

**$12,000 in potential bookings just didn't walk through the door.**

Megan pulls up her phone to check if she sent the reminder email. She sent one — three days ago.
She meant to send another yesterday but got caught in back-to-back calls about the Henderson
wedding next Friday.

*If I had sent the reminder yesterday, would they have shown up? Probably.*

---

## 10:00 AM — The Tour

The 10 AM couple arrives — on time. Emily and Jake, 28 and 31, clearly have been doing their
research. They're carrying a three-ring binder. Jake's phone is out recording B-roll of the
ceremony space.

*Good sign. They're serious.*

Sarah walks them through the ceremony lawn, the cocktail hour pavilion, the grand reception hall.
She's done this 200 times. She knows which light hits the altar at 4:30 PM in October.

Emily asks the question Sarah's been waiting for: *"Can you tell us about pricing?"*

Sarah feels the familiar tension. Her packages start at $9,500 and run to $22,000 for the full
buyout. She's been coached — by an old venue consultant, by a Facebook group thread from 2019 —
to never give the number until they've fallen in love with the space.

*What if we'd shown the pricing on the website? Would they have come if they knew the number?
Would it have weeded out the couples who toured last month and disappeared after seeing the
contract?*

She gives the range. Emily's face is neutral. Jake nods. They take her information packet.

---

## 12:30 PM — The BEO Update

Between tours, Sarah sits at the venue office laptop and opens a sprawling spreadsheet. The
Henderson wedding — 180 guests, next Friday — needs a final BEO review. She cross-references
the guest count update she got Tuesday (they went from 172 to 183), adjusts the F&B minimum
calculation, and fires off an updated document to the caterer via email.

She waits for confirmation.

The caterer has a different version of the BEO. This is the third reconciliation this week.

*If there were one document both of us were editing live, I'd have gotten 45 minutes of my life
back today.*

---

## 2:00 PM — The Tour That Doesn't Show

2 PM comes and goes.

By 2:20, Megan texts: *"Should I call them?"*

*Yes. No. What's the point.* Sarah texts back: "Give it ten more minutes."

At 2:35 the couple texts Megan — not Sarah — from a number not in the CRM: *"So sorry, we
ended up going with a venue in Dripping Springs last night. We forgot to cancel."*

Megan walks into the main house with her phone out. She doesn't have to say anything. Sarah
knows.

**One tour slot. 90 minutes of prep. One no-show.** That's $8,000–$14,000 in potential booking
revenue. Gone. And they chose the other venue *last night* — they had 14 hours to cancel.

*What if there had been a reminder that fired this morning? Not from me — automated. "Hey, your
tour is in 6 hours! Here's the parking code and what to bring." Would they have felt too guilty
to ghost?*

Probably, yes.

---

## 4:00 PM — The Follow-Up Queue

The 4 PM is Megan's task: follow up with all open inquiries from the past two weeks. She opens
the spreadsheet. Seventeen names. Color coded — red for no response after 48 hours, yellow for
one reply, green for tour scheduled.

Eight are red.

Megan types personalized emails. She checks Instagram to see if any of them posted about venue
shopping — one did, three days ago. She mentions it casually in the email.

*This would take 20 minutes if there were templates that actually sounded like us.*

It takes 90 minutes. Three of the eight reds will never reply. Two will book tours. Three will
respond with "we went with someone else" or nothing at all.

**Coordinator time cost: 90 minutes. Revenue recovered: maybe $16,000 if both tours convert.
But this happens every single week, and Megan's getting tired of it.**

---

## 6:30 PM — The End-of-Day Review

Sarah sits on the venue's back porch with a glass of wine, laptop open. She has 11 open
inquiries in various stages. She has two January Sundays she cannot fill. She has a coordinator
who works 50 hours a week and is starting to look at other job postings.

Her phone shows one new Google review — 5 stars from the Morales wedding two weekends ago.

*That's two reviews this month. We need 200 to compete with Vineyard on the Hill.*

She makes a mental note to ask Megan to email every couple from the past year asking for a
review. She made this same mental note last month.

*If this happened automatically — three days after the wedding, personalized, perfect timing —
we'd have 15 reviews this month instead of two.*

She closes the laptop. She'll be back here at 6:47 AM tomorrow.

---

## The Dream System Close

In the ideal world, Sarah wakes up to three **pre-qualified** inquiries — couples who've already
entered their budget range, confirmed the date, and answered the guest count question before she
ever touched the email. Her tour calendar shows zero no-shows because a five-touch reminder
sequence fired automatically — text, email, text, text, email — over the five days before each
appointment. Her January open dates are being actively promoted to last year's warm leads with
a time-sensitive discount. And at 9 AM, two new Google reviews came in from Saturday's wedding,
because the system fired a perfectly timed message to both couples on Tuesday morning.

Sarah's coordinator works 40 hours a week instead of 55. The business grows. The venue gets the
reputation it deserves.

That system exists. It just hasn't been sold to Sarah yet.

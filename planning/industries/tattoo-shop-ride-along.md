# A Day in the Shoes of a Tattoo Shop Owner

---

## 10:02 AM – Pulling Up the DMs Before I'm Even Out of the Car

I sit in the parking lot for four minutes before I go inside. Not because I'm lazy. Because once I walk through that door I don't stop moving until 9 PM and I've learned to do the DM triage out here where it's quiet.

Thirty-one messages since last night. I scroll fast. Three people asking "how much for a small tattoo" — I archived those without answering because that question has no answer and anyone who's been tattooing for more than six months knows it. Two people asking if I'm taking walk-ins today. One person who sent eleven reference photos and a paragraph that starts "so I was thinking something like this but more personal." One person confirming their 1 PM. One person who was supposed to come in at noon yesterday and is now asking if they can reschedule.

*Thinking: "She didn't cancel. She just disappeared and is now acting like we rescheduled. That's a hundred and fifty dollars I blocked out for nothing yesterday."*

I type a polite reply. I move her to next Thursday. I don't charge her the deposit forfeit because I still don't have a system that makes it not-awkward, so I just eat it. Again.

---

## 10:18 AM – The Shop Opens. The Day Starts Lying to Me.

My artist Marcus is already here, setting up his station. He's got a full book today — 11 AM, 2 PM, 5 PM. Those are his clients. Mine starts at 1.

I make coffee and open Square on the iPad behind the counter. Yesterday's sales are there. Two pieces, one deposit collected in cash, one via Square. The cash deposit is written on a Post-It note I stuck to the monitor. *Thinking: "I've been meaning to put that in the system for three days."*

The front window has our hours on it. Walk-ins welcome. I typed that myself on a Canva template in 2022. I keep meaning to update it.

---

## 10:47 AM – The Walk-In Who Wants a Sleeve Quote in Twelve Minutes

A guy walks in. Mid-twenties. He's been thinking about a half sleeve, he says. Black and grey. Traditional Japanese. Or maybe neo-trad. He shows me his arm. Then he shows me a screenshot of someone else's arm. Then a second screenshot. Then a video.

I spend twelve minutes with him. He's a good client — actually has vision, actually has budget. But he wants to talk and I have DMs backing up and a 1 PM consult I still need to pull reference for.

*Thinking: "I need to send him a form. Something that captures all of this before he walks in. Something that lets me prep instead of improvise."*

I don't have that form. I give him my Instagram handle and tell him to DM me so we can talk timeline. He nods. He will probably DM me at 11 PM.

**Walk-in:** "Should I just like... message you?"
**Me:** "Yeah, DM works best."

*Thinking: "That's how I lose people. Every 'DM me' is a handoff I can't control."*

---

## 11:30 AM – Marcus's Client Is a No-Show

11 AM client. Still no call, no text. Marcus checks his phone. Nothing. He shrugs, pulls up his portfolio on Instagram, starts editing photos. That's his coping mechanism. Mine is standing at the counter doing nothing while silently calculating.

Three hours minimum, $200/hour minimum. That's at least $600 Marcus was supposed to make today, $240 of which comes to the shop on a 40/60 split. Gone. Not rescheduled, gone — because his client already paid a $75 deposit and forfeiting it stings enough that they usually just ghost.

*Thinking: "Seventy-five dollars is supposed to protect a six-hundred-dollar time block. That math has never worked and I've known it for two years."*

I don't adjust the deposit. I don't know how to have that conversation with clients without sounding like a greedy shop. I tell myself I'll figure it out later.

---

## 1:05 PM – My Consult. Five Minutes Late. Already Derailed.

Her name is Kayla. She wants a botanical piece on her ribs. She sent me fifteen images on Instagram — I scrolled through them at 10 AM in the parking lot and thought I had a read on what she wants. I don't. The fifteen images are three completely different aesthetics. Fine line. Blackwork. Watercolor. She wants "all of them but cohesive."

I sketch for twenty minutes. She changes her mind twice. By 1:40 I have something that might work and she's nodding but not fully committing.

*Thinking: "I should have had a form that asked: pick one style. Pick one size. Pick a placement and don't change it at the consult."*

She leaves without booking. She says she'll think about it. Those three words are the sound of a piece I'll never do.

That's maybe $600–$900 walkout value, plus the forty minutes of my time.

---

## 2:30 PM – The Inbox Is a Disaster Again

I have a method. Check DMs at 10 AM, check again at 2 PM, check again at close. It's not working. Between 1:00 and 2:30 I got nine new messages. Three are clients currently on my books. Two are from people I've never talked to. One is a vendor. One is a person asking if I do cover-ups (yes). One is someone who wants me to "take a look at this design their friend drew." One is Marcus's 2 PM confirming they're on the way.

I answer them in no particular order, typing on my phone while standing at the counter, losing track of who I've replied to. I accidentally leave one on read and don't notice until 8 PM.

*Thinking: "There has to be a better way to do this. Any other booking-based business in the world has a form. A link. Something structured. Why am I still doing this in the comments section of my own life."*

---

## 4:15 PM – My Actual Session. This Is the Good Part.

For the next three hours I'm just an artist. That's the part I signed up for. A regular client, back for the third session on a back piece. She knows the process, she's got the playlist ready, she's hydrated. She doesn't talk too much. I get into flow within twenty minutes.

No DMs. No deposits. No consults. Just line weight and contrast ratios and the specific kind of quiet that only exists when the work is going well.

*Thinking: "This is why I do this. Not the admin. Not the no-shows. This."*

---

## 7:30 PM – End of Session. Back to Reality.

She loves it. Healed photo in six weeks, I remind her. She nods. She pays. She tips. She walks out and I am already looking at the counter.

There's a Post-It with a cash deposit from this morning still stuck to the monitor. Marcus's no-show client never reached back out. The walk-in from this morning never DMed. The consult walkout, gone. I got one message at 3 PM that I forgot to answer.

I count the day in my head: Two completed sessions (mine and Marcus's 2 PM and 5 PM). One no-show. One consult walkout. Roughly $240–$360 in lost shop revenue, plus forty minutes of un-billed consult time, plus the original hundred and fifty from the no-show yesterday I never charged.

*Thinking: "Every single one of those was preventable if I had the right system."*

---

## 9:10 PM – Closing. The Wishlist Comes Back.

I lock up. I sit in my car for a minute — same spot as this morning, different energy. I have forty-one DMs I haven't fully processed. I have four open booking conversations that haven't converted. I have a Post-It note on my monitor with a cash deposit I still haven't entered.

Marcus is booked through the next six weeks. I've got three weeks solid and gaps after that. That's the gap I need to close.

*Thinking: "I didn't get into tattooing to spend four hours a day on my phone. There's a version of this job where I just show up and tattoo. Someone build me that."*

---

## THE DREAM SYSTEM

If I could design the perfect system for my shop, here's what it would do.

### Before the day starts:
- Every open booking inquiry from the night before would already have an intake form submitted — style preference, placement, size range, reference images (max 6), budget tier, skin tone. I look at it over coffee. I show up prepped, not improvising.
- My daily schedule would be confirmed, deposited, and locked. No mystery about who's coming in and who isn't.
- Any no-show risk — someone who hasn't opened their reminder in 48 hours — flagged automatically so I can send a manual nudge.

### While I'm slammed during the day:
- Walk-ins get handed a QR code at the counter. They fill out the same intake form on their phone while they wait. By the time I talk to them, I already have their skin type, their reference, their budget. No twelve-minute fishing expedition.
- DMs get auto-replied with a booking link. "Hey — to get on my books, start here." Not a cold link. A warm one that shows my portfolio, my style filters, my available windows.
- Deposits are real. Configurable forfeit window. Client gets an email at 48 hours: "Your deposit becomes a credit when you show up, and is forfeited if you cancel inside 24 hours." No conversation needed. The system says it.

### End of day:
- Every completed session triggers an automatic aftercare text within the hour. Seven-day check-in. Six-week healed photo request. All passive. I never have to remember.
- The day's revenue — deposits, sessions, tip tracking — is already in one place. No Post-Its. No mental math in the parking lot.
- My pipeline is visible: who's a confirmed booking, who's a consult I'm waiting on, who ghosted. I can see in thirty seconds where my next three weeks stand.

### Old way vs. dream system:

**Old way:**
- DMs are my CRM, my booking system, and my marketing channel simultaneously
- Deposits are collected but rarely enforced — the awkwardness is mine to manage
- I find out about no-shows when the time slot starts and nobody walks in
- Consult walkovers have no follow-up because I have no system to follow up

**Dream system:**
- Intake happens before the conversation — I'm prepped, not reactive
- Deposits enforce themselves — the policy is in the confirmation, not in an uncomfortable conversation
- No-shows trigger an automatic forfeit and an automatic rebooking prompt
- Consult walkovers get a 48-hour "still thinking about it?" follow-up without me lifting a finger

That's a realistic day in a tattoo shop owner's shoes — and the gap between what they have and what they're dreaming about is exactly one well-built booking system away.
